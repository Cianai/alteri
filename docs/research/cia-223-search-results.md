## Key Patterns & Gaps Identified

### Methodological Advances
1. **Probe-based approaches:** Jacobsen et al. (2025) provides direct evidence that theory-based interview probes (descriptive, idiographic, clarifying, explanatory) enhance survey data quality.
2. **Preference for conversational formats:** Weisenburger et al. (2024) shows users prefer AI conversational assessments over traditional scales.
3. **Adaptive multi-turn dialogues:** Multiple 2024-2025 papers address consistency and adaptation in multi-turn conversations.

### Ego Network Specific
1. **Classic web-based methods:** Vehovar et al. (2008) remains foundational for web questionnaire design but predates modern LLMs.
2. **Name generator simplification:** Hampton (2007, cited in SAGE Handbook) explored alternatives to traditional multiple name generators.
3. **Cognitive issues:** Research exists on how respondents interpret hierarchical name generators (Antonucci approach).

### Technical Infrastructure
1. **Goal-oriented dialogue datasets:** MultiWOZ, Doc2Dial, MutualFriends provide structured dialogue examples.
2. **Interview-style generation:** Qwen2.5-based models fine-tuned for interview dialogue generation.
3. **Structured extraction:** Multiple frameworks for extracting structured data from conversations (Wu et al. 2019; Choubey et al. 2025).

### Gaps for CIA-223
1. **No direct LLM + ego network studies:** Search found no papers specifically combining LLM conversational agents with ego network name generators.
2. **Limited adaptive name generator work:** Most adaptive questionnaire research focuses on mental health or general surveys, not network elicitation.
3. **Missing comparison studies:** No studies comparing traditional name generators to LLM-based conversational approaches for ego network data.
